### Copper Quest Exploration Inc.

Completes and AI-driven geological analysis of the Kitimat Cu-Au Project, Kitimat, BC

<div class="note-block">
<strong>Note:</strong><br>

'Confirming a large conductive anomaly consistent with a buried porphyry center' lacks drilling. 'Very large', 'significant', and 'near-surface' lack metrics. 'Open' lacks geological context. Kitimat intervals lack 'from' distances. The figure indicates that the Jeanette Zone is a low-sulfidation deposit model. Core size, sample length, true width, collar table, prep & assay method, generative AI probabilities, and a QA/QC description are missing. Intervals lack Maximum Internal Dilution and minimum grade. <br>
The AI-modelling identified a 1.5 km by 1.5 km conductive body with vertical continuity to 1 km. While the platform uses a probabilistic 3D framework to rank scenarios, several high-level technical risks persist: <br>
Interpretive Offset Risk: The post-AI interpretation of the 2010 Jeannette Zone drilling (within a phyllic-potassic halo in ARIS 31807) is that it is a "peripheral expression" of a newly identified central conductor. However, the accuracy of this "offset" depends entirely on the spatial precision of the geophysical inversion. <br>
Autocorrelation & Verification: Without new drilling, the AI risks autocorrelation — the ranking of clusters by correlating multiple datasets (VTEM, Mags, & surface Geochem) that are mathematically capable of reflecting the same non-unique surface features. <br>
Inversion Non-Uniqueness: <br>
- Conductivity targets lack volume/depth validation due to inversion non-uniqueness, limited depth resolution beyond 1-2 skin depths (~500m), and the inability to measure true thickness — a small, highly conductive object (like a narrow vein of metal) can produce the same signal as a large, weakly conductive object (like a wide zone of salty water or clay). <br>
- Magnetic targets lack volume/depth validation due to inversion non-uniqueness, limited depth resolution, and an inability to measure true thickness. A small, highly magnetic body at depth (e.g., a narrow magnetite-rich vein) can produce the same magnetic anomaly as a much larger, weakly magnetic body closer to surface (e.g., a broad zone of weakly magnetized rock). <br>
Integrating historical data into a geophysically-heavy, unified framework creates a mathematically precise model, but it remains a statistical hypothesis. Until the conductive source is physically intersected, the AI is unlikely to distinguish between an economic sulphide core and barren conductive cover. <br>
